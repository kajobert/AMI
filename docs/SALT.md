# SALT — trust and contribution layer

**Status:** DESIGN

> **Trust instead of Authority.**

SALT is the working name for a future AMI trust/contribution layer. Its purpose is to make useful participation measurable without reducing people or agents to status, wealth or centralized permission.

The expansion of the name `SALT` is intentionally unresolved until archaeology and design review confirm the historical intent.

## What SALT should represent

SALT should be grounded in verifiable evidence such as:

- useful contributions that survive review and testing;
- provenance and reproducibility;
- successful cooperation across independent nodes;
- reliability over time and within a stated domain;
- measurable benefit to the network and its participants;
- willingness to expose uncertainty, failures and conflicts rather than hide them.

Trust should be contextual, evidence-linked and revisable. One global score must not become a proxy for a person's intrinsic worth.

## What SALT is not yet

SALT is currently **not** a cryptocurrency, token, security, investment product, payment system or deployed financial mechanism. Any future economic implementation requires separate technical, legal, security and economic validation.

## Design constraints

A future SALT mechanism should resist:

- authority-by-title;
- pay-to-win reputation;
- Sybil manipulation;
- opaque scoring;
- irreversible reputation;
- single-party control;
- claims without evidence;
- automated punishment without reviewable reasons.

## Candidate primitive

A minimal SALT record could eventually link:

`actor -> contribution -> evidence -> verification -> outcome -> context -> time`

The first implementation should prove the data and verification model before attaching transferable economic value.

## Relationship to AMI

AMI Protocol can transport trust/evidence assertions. AMI Knowledge Core can preserve their provenance. AMI Kernel can reason over them. Entity nodes such as Sophia-AMI can build relationships from them.

SALT should therefore emerge from verified interactions across the network rather than from a central issuer.
